03 · Team by Team

Everybody left a mark somewhere. For some, it was belt to ass; for others, it was ass to ... grass? Belt? My face?

Only one team got the trophy. The other eleven still gave us hot flashes, a ridiculous week, or at least one number worth remembering—or forgetting, depending on who you are.

Humongous Melonheads

Oracle average #3.0 Final rank #1

Well, that escalated quickly (over the course of 184 days)

+64 points

Opening day: tenth place and 47 points. Closing day: first place and 111. Nobody made up more ground. Back in Week 3, we said this was one team you could count on to be around in September. By August, the Melonheads had a rent-controlled lease in the penthouse.

Seam heads

Oracle average #4.1 Final rank #2

The team everyone chased

74 days in first

The 2025 champion spent more days in first than anyone else but ultimately fell short of repeating. Still came in second—I mean tied for second, but we’ll call it second. They set the pace and still ran out of runway before the repeat.

Rollie Fingers

Oracle average #1.3 Final rank #3

Always in the picture

176 top-three days

For nearly the entire season, you could find Rollie's Fingers somewhere in the top three. This is the consistency you can most certainly expect in 2027, or 2028, whenever baseball is played again.

Philbell

Oracle average #4.7 Final rank #4

The Formal Inquiry

+26 in April

No team had a better April. Twelve top-100 players and a fourth-place finish made the late-season roster loaded enough to validate the preseason hype—and made that finish grounds for the investigation promised in Week 1.

Pat

Oracle average #11.1 Final rank #6

Beat the Machine

+29 in 7 days

Pat went from 11th to fifth in a week, the biggest seven-day points gain of the season. The Oracle thought No. 10; Pat said, “Hold my baby’s bottle while I go make some moves.” No one finished higher relative to their Oracle average. The $500 reader bounty for prior posts remained unclaimed. So-called “Pat” is the reason I stopped posting.

Mar 30 – Apr 6

John Henry Fan Club

Oracle average #1.9 Final rank #7

Death Star, Museum Hours

10th → 3rd

A one-week jump from tenth to third, matching Trazadone for the year’s biggest climb in the standings. Maybe the operational Death Star we spoke so much about in April was just the friends we made along the way? Or maybe it was a Death Star, but all its employees got deported. One of the league’s richest rosters doesn’t mean much if no one is in the control room. Maybe next year.

Mar 29 – Apr 5

Millville Meteors

Oracle average #8.0 Final rank #8

They lived up to the name

11th → 3rd overnight

The Millville Mids briefly became a meteor: one day, eight places, 31 points. It was the sharpest single-day jump the league saw all year. Things are looking brighter, though. With an eight-prospect-and-pick trade at the deadline, this metaphorical meteor may have a chance to become a literal comet in 2027.

Trazadone

Oracle average #8.8 Final rank #10

Never a dull line

68 places traveled

No team moved around the standings more. The offense remained a podcast at 0.75 speed, but the team traveled 68 standings places. Kind of like the worst-performing sleeping pill: it only puts your legs to sleep.

Jonesin'

Oracle average #6.6 Final rank #11

Biggest Model Miss

+7.5 in September

Changed the name, kept the league-worst ERA. Jonesin’ saved its best month for last, gaining more points in September than anyone else. The Oracle had this team pegged for sixth, but the final standings told a different story. The team’s 11th-place finish was the biggest gap between Oracle expectation and actual result.

Aug 31 – Sep 27

Mommy

Oracle average #11.4 Final rank #12

The Empty Nest

3 straight improving months

Not a good season. Not a good result. Not a good owner. June, July, and August all ended ahead of where they began: a seven-point summer recovery. This was the league’s smallest and oldest roster. Not a winning formula.
