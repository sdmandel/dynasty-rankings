This week the power rankings switch engines. Instead of leaning on the dynasty Oracle, the order now blends three things: your season body of work, your recent form, and a rest-of-season projection of the players actually on your active roster right now — injured-list and minor-league stashes excluded. The biggest consequence: philbell falls three on a top-four standings line because its active roster projects near the bottom, while Mommy jumps five on the back of two scorching bats. Seam Heads and Rollie Fingers still run 1–2. How it works is in the box below.

New This Week
How These Rankings Work

Power rankings now run on a three-pillar model: season body of work (40%), recent form (30%, recency-weighted and regressed toward the season so one hot week can’t take over), and rest-of-season outlook (25%) — a live projection of each team’s active MLB roster, with IL and minor-league stashes excluded. A small narrative term (5%) credits time spent atop the standings. The dynasty Oracle now informs but no longer drives the weekly order. The Projected Final Standings table and these Power Rankings are two outputs of the same engine — they share inputs but weight them differently, so a team can rank higher in one than the other.

Projected Final Standings

The Team Intel page projects each team’s finish from a live rest-of-season projection of its active roster, with injured-list and minor-league stashes excluded so paper depth stops flattering the contenders. It’s the same outlook pillar that feeds the rankings above.
Manager Engagement — Week 12 Watch

Lineup sessions counts distinct edit sessions: each time a manager touches their lineup counts once, regardless of how many moves were made in that session. Avg/Wk is season sessions over twelve weeks; Wk 12 is this week alone. Moves is season adds plus drops. Trades is season trades completed.
